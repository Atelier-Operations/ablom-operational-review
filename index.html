01

The business model

ABLOM is a venture studio for creator-led brands: it builds and co-owns product companies with creators, rather than selling them a service.

Concretely, ABLOM identifies creators who already have audience trust but no operational infrastructure, then supplies what they are missing, including ops, supply chain, legal, ecom backend, and brand development, to turn that audience into an owned, sellable brand.

The four-step build

01

Scouting

Identify creators with strong audience trust and product potential.

02

Co-building

ABLOM supplies the core team, covering ops, brand, product, legal, and ecom.

03

Incubation & distribution

Launch DTC via the creator's own channels, including TikTok Shop, LIVE, and affiliate.

04

Shared upside

ABLOM holds equity or revenue share in the brand's long-term success.

How ABLOM gets paid

10–30%

Equity / revenue share

Per brand co-built with a creator. This is the core bet, a portfolio play rather than a single-business one.

10–25%

Ecom backend margin

Sourcing, fulfillment, logistics services charged regardless of how the brand performs.

% of deal

Licensing & IP

Once a brand has traction, license it into new categories or markets.

In short, ABLOM is less a marketing agency and more a hybrid startup studio and talent agency, betting that a few brands in the portfolio become genuinely valuable, the way a VC fund bets on a portfolio rather than one company.

02

Target: who ABLOM builds with

The stated focus is the top 1% of diaspora creators, who are Black and Arab, Anglophone and Francophone, with global resonance and commercial ambition.

$10K+/month income 300K–2M audience France · UK · US · Canada Wants to own a brand, not just promote one

Worth naming plainly: the actual example creators referenced (Funmi Monet, Laura Ikeji, Laylah Ali Othman, Adanna Steinacker) all already have an existing product or brand of some kind, such as a perfume line, a beauty brand, a furniture business, or a consulting platform. That is a different business than co-creating a brand from zero with a creator who has none, and it is worth resolving which one this actually is before targeting.

03

The three verticals on the table

Cosmetics, home & living, and fine dining were all raised as potential launch categories. They do not share a supply chain, a regulatory framework, or a launch playbook, and trying to run all three in parallel from day one multiplies operational complexity.

VerticalCore operational demandWhat needs to be done
Cosmetics
  • Formulation or private-label sourcing
  • Safety assessment
  • Ingredient compliance (EU CPNP, US MoCRA)
  • Packaging
  • Small-batch manufacturing
  • Select a formulation/private-label partner
  • Complete the safety assessment file
  • Register a responsible person / CPNP
  • Lock packaging spec
  • Run a small-batch production trial
Home & living
  • Freight-heavy sourcing
  • Longer lead times
  • Breakage and logistics risk
  • Furniture-grade QC
  • Identify manufacturing partners
  • Negotiate freight and logistics terms
  • Define QC and breakage protocols
  • Map customs/import requirements per market
Fine dining
  • Curating culinary products made by well-known, established chefs
  • Bringing their sauces, condiments, spice blends, or signature goods directly to a wider audience
  • Sign chef partners
  • Select a co-packer or food manufacturer
  • Obtain food safety certification and labeling compliance
  • Define shelf life and packaging
  • Set up distribution

Rather than position one over the others, I'd frame the choice around a few criteria.

  • 01
    Existing traction Is there already a pilot, a creator, or a real pipeline in that category?
  • 02
    Time to first sale How long from creator sign-on to a sellable product, given sourcing and regulatory lead times?
  • 03
    Regulatory complexity How much compliance work stands between sign-on and launch?
  • 04
    Capital required Working capital needed for a first production run and initial fulfillment setup.
  • 05
    Creator fit Which category matches the audience and product interest of the creators already engaged?

Whichever comes out ahead on these is the one worth building the operational backbone around first.

04

Open questions

A few things I'd want to align on before we go further. Most of these are natural at this stage, and probably already on your mind.

Question: current state

What exists already? Is there a pipeline?

Where things actually stand right now: any creators already in conversation, any early commitments, anything sourced or tested so far. Would help calibrate the real starting point.

Question: vision

What's the long-term vision for ABLOM itself?

Whether the ambition is a handful of well-run brands, a much larger portfolio, or eventually an exit for the studio itself. This shapes how much infrastructure is worth building now versus later.

Question: timeline

What's the roadmap beyond the September start?

First creator signed by when, first product live by when, and when the second and third verticals would come into play. Helps calibrate urgency and sequencing.

Question: targets

What are the targets for year one?

Number of creators signed, brands launched, and revenue or portfolio value expected. Gives a concrete bar to build the operational backbone against.

Question: model shape

Zero-to-one, or scaling an existing brand?

Every named example creator already has a product or brand. Whether ABLOM is meant to build from nothing, or professionalize what already exists informally, since they would call for different playbooks and timelines.

Question: core team

Who's already on the ABLOM team itself?

Founders, current hires, and the hiring plan for the shared core team, distinct from the personal team assigned to each creator. Would help calibrate what's already in place versus what still needs building.

Question: execution team

Who's slated to run brand ops day to day?

The model describes a personal team per creator, including a brand manager, an ops lead, and a content strategist. Where that stands in terms of hiring, and what the plan is before the first creator signs.

Question: seller of record

Who's legally selling the product?

ABLOM, the creator's own entity, or a shared structure? This shapes who carries product liability, tax obligations, and returns, and it is worth settling before the first sale, not after.

Question: marketing & CX

Who owns marketing and customer experience once live?

Beyond the creator's organic audience, who handles paid marketing, customer service, and returns? Worth being explicit so nothing falls into a gap between ABLOM and the creator.

Question: funding

What's the plan on capital?

The Vision/Ask section is still open in the deck. A studio model needs capital to get built, covering the shared operational layer and each brand's working capital, before revenue comes in. How you're thinking of financing that, and the timeline relative to September.

Question: advisory scope

What does the advisory board role look like in practice?

Quarterly strategic input, or something closer to hands-on build?

05

The operational backbone I imagine

This is the same discipline regardless of which vertical is live. What changes is the specific partners and regulations, not the method. It is the piece that turns co-build from a slide into something repeatable, and it is what I have built at ANKA, Cdiscount/Octopia, and now Yes Conciergerie.

01

Partner sourcing & vetting

  • Identify and evaluate manufacturing, sourcing, or supplier partners against real criteria: capability, capacity, lead times, and quality track record
  • Build a shortlist of 3 to 5 candidates, with references checked, before anyone signs anything
02

Terms negotiation & leverage

  • Lock commercial terms that improve as volume grows across the portfolio: MOQs, payment terms, tiered pricing
  • This is where ABLOM's scale actually pays off, brand by brand, instead of each creator negotiating alone
03

Compliance & regulatory mapping

  • Map exactly what's legally required before anything launches
  • The specific rules change per category, but the discipline of mapping them early stays the same
  • Built once as a checklist, reused every time
04

Launch runbook & QC

  • A real operational sequence with clear gates from sign-on to live
  • Reorder and demand logic that keeps a good launch from becoming a mess once it actually sells
05

Inventory & demand planning

  • Set reorder points and safety stock levels before launch, not after the first stockout
  • Forecast demand against the creator's promotional calendar: drops, LIVE events, affiliate pushes
  • Track sell-through by SKU to catch slow movers before they tie up cash
06

Reporting & performance tracking

  • Build a simple dashboard covering margin, fulfillment cost, and revenue share per brand
  • Flag underperforming SKUs or partners early enough to act on them
  • Give ABLOM and the creator the same numbers, so conversations start from shared facts

Concretely, that means showing up with a short, prioritized list of vetted partners and real terms before a single creator signs, building the compliance file in parallel with product development rather than after, and having the reorder and inventory logic ready before the first spike happens, rather than improvised once it does.
